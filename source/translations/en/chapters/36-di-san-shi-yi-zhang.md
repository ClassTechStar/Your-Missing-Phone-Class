### Chapter 31: Getting Started with Short Video Shooting and Content Creation

{class="chapter-intro mb-4"}
In 2026, short videos are no longer "toys for young people" — they've become the fundamental way everyone gets information and expresses themselves. Whether you want to document your life, share knowledge, or start a side business, your phone is your best creative tool — no need to buy a camera or learn Premiere. A single phone is enough to get started. This chapter helps you start from scratch.

#### I. Think Before You Shoot: Who's Your Audience?

The first mistake many people make is "picking up the phone and shooting immediately" without knowing who the audience is. Before you start, answer three questions:

1. <strong>Who is my audience?</strong>— Content for kids, peers, or elderly viewers requires completely different styles.
2. <strong>What value can I provide?</strong>— Make people laugh (entertainment), learn (knowledge), crave (food), or feel warm (emotion). You need at least one.
3. <strong>Which platform can I consistently post on?</strong>— Douyin/Kuaishou (general entertainment), Xiaohongshu (lifestyle), Bilibili (in-depth content), WeChat Channels (social circle). Choose one main platform to focus on — don't distribute across all platforms from the start.

#### II. Phone Camera Basic Settings — 5 Things to Do Before Shooting

1. <strong>Set Resolution to 1080P, Frame Rate to 30fps</strong>: This is the standard for short video platforms. 4K files are too large and upload slowly. 60fps has no advantage on social platforms and just creates bigger files.
2. <strong>Clean the Lens</strong>: The first and most important tip. Wipe the lens with your clothes before shooting — the image immediately becomes noticeably clearer.
3. <strong>Lock Focus and Exposure</strong>: Long-press the subject in the viewfinder for 2 seconds until "AE/AF Lock" appears. This prevents brightness fluctuations while moving during the shot.
4. <strong>Horizontal or Vertical?</strong>— For Douyin/Kuaishou/WeChat Channels/Xiaohongshu <strong>shoot vertically</strong> (9:16). For Bilibili/YouTube <strong>shoot horizontally</strong> (16:9). Decide the platform first, then the orientation.
5. <strong>Enable Grid Lines</strong>: Settings &gt; Camera &gt; Grid. The foundation of rule-of-thirds composition, helping you quickly find the visual center.

#### III. Five Camera Movement Techniques — Make Your Footage Come Alive

The biggest problem for beginner videographers is footage that's too "static" — standing still the entire time. Learn these five basic camera movements, and your footage will instantly feel more professional:

{class="list-disc pl-6 mb-3 space-y-2"}
- <strong>Push</strong>: The phone slowly moves toward the subject. Creates tension or intimacy of "approaching."
- <strong>Pull</strong>: The phone slowly moves away from the subject. Pulls from detail to wide shot, establishing the environment.
- <strong>Pan</strong>: The phone stays in place, rotating left or right. Good for landscapes and showing space.
- <strong>Track</strong>: The phone moves parallel, following the subject. Requires steady hands, best with a smartphone gimbal.
- <strong>Follow</strong>: The phone follows behind or in front of the subject. The most commonly used movement in vlogs.

<strong>Stability Is the Top Priority</strong>: Shaky footage looks very unprofessional. Two-handed grip is more stable than one-handed; when walking, bend your knees slightly and use a "ninja walk" to reduce up-and-down bouncing; if you have budget, buy a smartphone gimbal (DJI OM series about 500-800 yuan) — it's a game-changer.

#### IV. Editing Basics — CapCut Is Enough

JianYing (the domestic version of CapCut) is currently the most beginner-friendly mobile editing tool — free, feature-rich, and template-heavy. Here's the basic workflow:

1. <strong>Import Footage</strong>: Open CapCut &gt; New Project &gt; Select and import your video clips.
2. <strong>Trim</strong>: Drag the timeline to trim the wasted footage at the beginning and end of each clip. Principle: keep only the parts with information — shorter is better than longer.
3. <strong>Add Transitions</strong>: Tap the "Transition" button between two clips and choose a simple transition effect. Beginners should use "Dissolve" or "Fade to Black" — avoid flashy 3D transitions.
4. <strong>Add Subtitles</strong>: CapCut has an "Auto Captions" feature that generates subtitles with one tap. Accuracy is about 90% — just manually proofread once.
5. <strong>Add Music</strong>: CapCut's music library has many royalty-free tracks. Choose a BGM that matches the rhythm and content, and set the volume to about 1/3 of the original video audio.
6. <strong>Export</strong>: Export at 1080P, 30fps, bitrate set to "Recommended."

#### V. The Importance of Thumbnails — 70% of People Decide Whether to Watch Based on the Cover

A short video's thumbnail is its "storefront" — it determines whether people click. Key points for making thumbnails:

- <strong>Thumbnail Should Have One Sentence</strong>: Write the core content of the video in large text, e.g., "Learn Phone Photo Editing in 3 Minutes." Font should be large, colors bright, with contrast against the background.
- <strong>Thumbnail Must Match Content</strong>: Don't be a "clickbaiter" — if the thumbnail says one thing but the video shows another, viewers will swipe away immediately, and the algorithm will penalize you.
- <strong>Facial Expressions Should Be Vivid</strong>: If the thumbnail has a person, pick a moment with a vivid or exaggerated expression. Human faces attract more clicks than text.
- <strong>Making Thumbnails in CapCut</strong>: In CapCut, select the best frame, add text overlay, and export as an image for the thumbnail.

#### VI. Three Content Creation Principles

{class="list-disc pl-6 mb-3 space-y-2"}
- <strong>First 3 Seconds Determine Life or Death</strong>: On short video platforms, viewers decide within 3 seconds whether to swipe or keep watching. The first 3 seconds must have a "hook" — a question, a suspense, or a surprising visual.
- <strong>One Video, One Topic</strong>: The most common mistake beginners make is cramming too much into one video. One video should solve one problem — thoroughness beats comprehensiveness.
- <strong>Persistence Beats Talent</strong>: Algorithmic recommendation requires "warming up the account." The first 20 videos will get almost no views — this is normal. Keep updating 2-3 per week, let the algorithm learn your content direction, and traffic will gradually grow.

<i class="fas fa-hands mr-2"></i>Hands-On: Shoot a 30-Second Short Video

{class="list-decimal pl-6 mt-1 space-y-1"}
1. Pick a small topic you know well (e.g., "How to make phone fonts larger").
2. Shoot 3-5 vertical clips, each 5-10 seconds.
3. Use CapCut to splice, trim, add subtitles, and add BGM.
4. Create a thumbnail with large text.
5. Export and send to a friend first for feedback before publishing.
