### Chapter 28: Spend Wisely on Your Dream Phone — The Ultimate Smartphone Buying Guide

{class="chapter-intro mb-4"}
When buying a phone, many people spend a week reading reviews, a month comparing prices, then end up spending five or six thousand on a flagship they don't actually need. This chapter doesn't list spec sheets — you can find those on official websites. What I want to give you is a "phone selection mindset" — so no matter what new phone hits the market, you can judge whether it suits you.

#### 0. Three-Step Phone Selection: Needs → Budget → Model

Many people ask online "what phone should I buy for 3,000 yuan?" — this question can't actually be answered, because we don't know what you'll primarily use the phone for. The correct thinking order when buying a phone should be:

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3 .mb-4
{class="font-semibold"}
<i class="fas fa-lightbulb mr-2"></i>Three-Step Phone Selection

{class="list-decimal pl-6 mt-1 space-y-1"}
1. <strong>First, figure out what you care about most</strong>: Photography? Gaming? Battery life? Screen? System smoothness? Or just need to make calls and use WeChat? Prioritize your needs.
2. <strong>Then, Set a Budget Ceiling</strong>: Not "roughly how much" but "the maximum I'm willing to spend." Set a hard ceiling so you don't keep looking at pricier options.
3. <strong>Finally, Pick a Model</strong>: Use your prioritized needs and budget to match with models.
:::

#### II. Budget-Tier Recommendations (Based on Mid-2026 Market)

The following recommendations are based on the mid-2026 Chinese mainland market. Prices are official starting prices. Actual prices fluctuate — refer to the price at time of purchase.

##### 💰 Under 1,000 Yuan: Can Make Calls, Use WeChat — Good Enough

<strong>Who It's For</strong>: Students, backup phones, users with minimal requirements, primarily for calls and WeChat.

<strong>What You Can Get</strong>: Redmi Note series, Honor X series, realme number series — low-to-mid configs. Processors are roughly Snapdragon 6-series or Dimensity 6000-series. Screens are mostly LCD. Cameras can take clear photos but autofocus is slow. System smoothness is average.

<strong>Buying Advice</strong>: At this price, look at two things: Is the battery big enough (choose 5000mAh+)? Is there enough memory (start with 6GB+128GB, don't buy 4GB — it'll lag)? Redmi has the clearest advantage at this price point.

##### 💰 1,000-2,000 Yuan: Best Value — The Sweet Spot That Lasts 3-4 Years Without Lag

<strong>Who It's For</strong>: Students, budget-conscious users who want longevity, no extreme demands on photography or gaming.

<strong>Representative Models</strong>: Redmi Turbo series, iQOO Z series, Honor X series high-spec, OnePlus Ace series entry-level.

<strong>Buying Advice</strong>: Choose models with Snapdragon 7+ Gen3 or Dimensity 7300+ processors, with 8GB+256GB memory. This configuration easily handles daily use + occasional Honor of Kings, and can last 3-4 years without lag.

##### 💰 2,000-3,500 Yuan: Mainstream — Noticeable Improvements in Performance and Photography

<strong>Who It's For</strong>: Ordinary users who want decent photography, lag-free gaming, and longevity. This is the price range most people buy in.

<strong>Representative Models</strong>: Xiaomi number series (like Xiaomi 17 standard after price drops), vivo S series high-spec, OPPO Reno series, Honor number series, Redmi K series.

<strong>Buying Advice</strong>: The biggest characteristic of this price range is "all-around phones" — no obvious weaknesses. Compared to sub-2,000 yuan phones, the main improvements are: noticeably better cameras (with OIS), high-quality OLED screens, faster charging (80W+ common), and more refined build quality. Choose 12GB+256GB or 12GB+512GB — easily lasts 4-5 years.

##### 💰 3,500-5,000 Yuan: Near-Flagship — More Than Enough for Everyone Except Enthusiasts

<strong>Who It's For</strong>: Users who pursue photography, those who want to experience premium build quality.

<strong>Representative Models</strong>: Xiaomi number series standard, vivo X series, OPPO Find X series standard, older iPhone flagships (like iPhone 16).

<strong>Buying Advice</strong>: At this price, you can already buy last year's flagship models. My advice is:<strong>Buy last year's flagship, not this year's mid-range</strong>. Last year's flagship typically has better materials, build, and camera hardware than this year's mid-range. The prerequisite is accepting it's not the "latest model."

##### 💰 5,000+ Yuan: Flagship Zone — Money No Object or Pursuing Ultimate Experience

<strong>Who It's For</strong>: Photography enthusiasts, heavy gamers, users seeking top-tier screens and craftsmanship, content creators who shoot video on phones.

<strong>Representative Models</strong>: iPhone 17 Pro/Pro Max, Samsung S26 Ultra, Huawei Pura 80 Ultra, Xiaomi 17 Ultra, vivo X300 Pro.

<strong>Buying Advice</strong>: At this price, the differences between brands are shrinking. It mainly comes down to which system you prefer. Like photography — choose vivo or Huawei. Like video — choose iPhone. Like screen and S Pen — choose Samsung. Like balance — choose Xiaomi. Honestly, at this price point,<strong>you can go with your gut — just pick the one that looks right to you</strong>.

#### IV. Five Common Pitfalls — Check Before You Buy

{class="list-decimal pl-6 mb-3 space-y-2"}
1. <strong>Pitfall 1: Four or Five Cameras Don't Equal Good Photos</strong>— Manufacturers love to stack lens numbers in specs, but two of them are often 2MP "filler lenses" (macro + depth) that are practically useless. When evaluating a phone's photography, <strong>only look at the main + ultrawide + telephoto</strong>— these three are enough. You can pretend the rest don't exist.
2. <strong>Pitfall 2: Don't Be Fooled by 108 Megapixels</strong>— High megapixel count doesn't mean good image quality. Sensor size, aperture, and algorithm tuning matter far more than pixel count. Many flagship phones use 50MP large-sensor cameras that produce photos far superior to some 108MP budget phones.
3. <strong>Pitfall 3: Don't Buy Carrier-Locked Phones</strong>— "Deposit phone credit, get a free phone" looks like a great deal, but carrier-customized phones have slow system updates, non-removable pre-installed apps, and are usually carrier-locked (only work with that carrier's SIM card). You think you're saving money, but you're actually getting "locked in."
4. <strong>Pitfall 4: "Curved Screens Are Premium" — Marketing, Not Truth</strong>— Curved screens were once a flagship signature, but more and more people are discovering their problems: hard to apply screen protectors, edge accidental touches, and expensive repairs when cracked. The 2026 trend is returning to "slightly curved" or even "completely flat" screens. Don't choose a curved screen just because "curved means premium."
5. <strong>Pitfall 5: Not All "AI Phones" Are Actually Useful</strong>— In 2026, every manufacturer is shouting "AI phone," but many just renamed their existing voice assistant. Genuinely useful AI features include: real-time translation, AI eraser (removing people from photos), AI photo editing, on-device LLM inference, and localized smart summaries. Other flashy features are mostly gimmicks.

#### V. When Is the Best Time to Buy?

- <strong>618 Shopping Festival (late May to mid-June)</strong>: The period with the biggest phone price drops of the year. Many manufacturers slash flagship prices by 800-1,500 yuan to boost sales. This is the best window for buying phones in the first half of the year.
- <strong>Double 11 (late October to mid-November)</strong>: The second-half mega sale, with discounts comparable to 618. Many brands release new flagships around this time, so the previous generation sees the biggest price drops.
- <strong>One Month After New Release</strong>: At launch it's full price, but after a month, channel prices (third-party stores) typically drop by 200-500 yuan.
- <strong>Two Weeks Before Chinese New Year to Lantern Festival</strong>: Brick-and-mortar stores clear inventory during the "New Year Goods Festival," and you can negotiate prices with staff.
- <strong>Not Recommended Times</strong>: Buying on launch day — unless you really want to be among the first owners, launch prices are the most expensive.

#### VI. Where to Buy — Which Channels Are Reliable?

- <strong>Brand Official Store / Tmall Flagship Store / JD.com Self-Operated</strong>: Safest option. No price advantage, but no worry about refurbished or counterfeit goods, and best after-sales service.
- <strong>Pinduoduo "10 Billion Subsidy"</strong>: Lowest prices, but only buy from stores labeled with "10 Billion Subsidy." Avoid unlabeled ones. Record an unboxing video when you receive the goods — you can return if there are issues.
- <strong>Physical Retail Stores</strong>: You can touch and feel the phone. Note: Sales staff will push high-margin models (not the most suitable ones for you). Before visiting, lock in 2-3 target models online, and tell them "I only want to see these."
- <strong>Used Platforms (Xianyu / Zhuanzhuan)</strong>: Cheaper but risky. If buying used, remember three rules: require purchase invoice or order screenshot, require IMEI number for verification, <strong>only use platform transactions, never transfer money privately</strong>.
