### Chapter 13: Recommended Utility Apps

{class="chapter-intro mb-4"}
This chapter picks productivity, health, and travel apps, plus practical system and cross-device tools: cleaners like Qingzhuo and spam blocking, built-in phone blocking switches, weather and earthquake alerts; remote desktop with Sunlogin/ToDesk, AI office tools like Trae, live wallpapers, LocalSend for LAN transfers, cross-device clipboard, Aegis/Microsoft Authenticator for one-time codes, TickTick schedules, and paper scanning.

#### 1. Productivity: Manage your time and attention

Productivity apps are the "backbone" of digital life. They help you organize scattered ideas, tasks, and files. Picks by scenario below.

##### 1. To-do lists (task management)

- TickTick. Best for: People who want work, life, and study tasks plus a calendar in one place. A top pick in this section. Highlights: Sync across phone/PC/tablet/web; to-dos + calendar + Pomodoro in one; natural language like "meeting tomorrow at 3pm" or "weekly report every Monday" auto-parses times and repeats; supports subtasks, tags, priorities, attachments, and shared lists. Great for "schedule + daily check-ins + review" in one flow.
- Getting more out of TickTick:
- Calendar view: Drag meetings and deadlines onto the calendar to spot conflicts at a glance — more intuitive than a plain list.
- Recurring tasks: Set meds, bills, and workouts as repeating rules instead of retyping every time.
- Pomodoro: Start focus sessions on a task and see how much you actually finished this week.
- Phone integration: Say "remind me…" to your voice assistant to create system reminders. Double up important deadlines in the system calendar too.

- Todoist. Best for: Minimalists who work across platforms. Highlights: Clean, restrained interface; clear hierarchy (task → subtask → sub-subtask); karma points that nudge good habits.
- Microsoft To Do. Best for: Office workers and Outlook users. Highlights: Completely free, no ads; "My Day" clears each morning and eases mental load; deep Microsoft ecosystem integration.
- System Notes/Reminders. Best for: Light users who'd rather not install third-party apps. Highlights: Native experience, zero lag, add via Siri/Xiao AI, works with system widgets.

##### 2. Pomodoro (focus training)

- Forest. Best for: People who can't keep their hands off their phone. Highlights: Grow a tree while you focus; leave the app early and the tree dies. Virtual life as positive feedback; plant together with friends.
- Tide. Best for: People who need white noise to focus or fall asleep. Highlights: Nature sounds (rain, waves, forest) + Pomodoro + meditation in one. Calm, healing, minimal interface.
- The rule of thumb: Classic 25 minutes focus + 5 minutes break. Adjust to 50/10 or 90/20 to match your energy.

##### 3. Calendars (visualize your time)

- System calendar. Best for: Everyone. Highlights: Most reliable native alerts, very low battery use, add via Siri/Xiao AI.
- Google Calendar. Best for: Multi-device users and shared schedules. Highlights: "Goals" auto-schedules fitness/study time; Gmail smartly pulls events from email.

##### 4. Notes (capture knowledge)

- Notion. Best for: People who want notes, tasks, databases, and wikis in one place — students, freelancers, teams. Highlights: All-in-one knowledge base with block editing; build homepages, reading lists, project boards; rich template ecosystem.
- Obsidian. Best for: Serious knowledge workers, researchers, writers. Highlights: Local Markdown storage, backlinks + graph view — let your notes form a "second brain." Your data is fully yours.
- flomo. Best for: Capturing quick ideas without writing long essays. Highlights: Card-style notes, as easy as posting a social update; # tags for grouping; browse by timeline.
- Apple Notes. Best for: iPhone/Mac users with light note-taking needs. Highlights: Fast launch; scans documents, sketches, checklists; iCloud sync. Free and deeply integrated.
- Evernote. Best for: Power users who clip lots of web pages and archive materials. Highlights: Powerful web clipper, OCR for text in images; long-standing and stable — but Premium is paid.

##### 5. PDF tools

- PDF Expert. Best for: iPad/iPhone users, professionals annotating contracts, students. Highlights: Widely considered the best PDF editor on iOS — smooth markup, signing, merge/split, form filling.
- WPS. Best for: Office users juggling Word/Excel/PPT/PDF. Highlights: One-stop office suite, plays well with Chinese cloud drives and WeChat files; free tier is plenty for daily use.
- Adobe Acrobat. Best for: People who need strict PDF compliance (legal, design, publishing). Highlights: Official standard, reliable PDF/A conversion, professional cross-device sync and e-signatures.

##### 6. Scanning (digitize paper)

Paper contracts, receipts, homework, whiteboard notes, ID copies… your phone camera can replace most scanners. Core flow: Shoot clearly → auto crop & perspective fix → export PDF/image → optional OCR.

- CamScanner. Best for: Business people and students who often scan contracts, receipts, and IDs. Highlights: Auto edge detection, perspective correction, OCR; multi-page PDFs with cloud sync.
- Microsoft Lens. Best for: Students who need whiteboards/pages as Word docs. Highlights: Free, no ads, one-tap export to OneNote/Word, accurate text recognition.
- Apple Notes scanning. Best for: iPhone users with light scanning needs. Highlights: No extra app — tap the camera icon in Notes; auto shadow removal, sign and markup. Free and solid.
- Baimiao / CamScanner web / brand camera "Document Scan": Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO, and vivo cameras mostly include a Documents/Scan mode. Android can also use Google Camera or the system scanner. Handy for snapping a one-page contract in a hurry.
- Shooting tips: Even lighting, keep the phone parallel to the page, aim at all four corners. For multi-page docs, shoot in order then merge to PDF. Watermark sensitive IDs and watch where you store them; clear them from "Recently Deleted" when done.

#### 2. Health and fitness

Health apps are the phone's most underrated "personal assistant." They track sleep, heart rate, and activity continuously — reflecting long-term trends better than the occasional hospital visit.

##### 1. Workouts

- Keep. Best for: Home workouts, total beginners, fat loss and toning. Highlights: Huge free course library from HIIT to yoga; AI builds your training plan; lively community.
- Nike Training Club (NTC). Best for: People who want professional coaching and structured training. Highlights: Official Nike app, all courses free, demos by real coaches, organized by muscle group/equipment.
- Apple Fitness. Best for: Apple Watch users who want ring-based motivation. Highlights: Three daily activity rings (Move/Exercise/Stand), visual goals, seamless heart-rate data from the watch.

##### 2. Sleep tracking

- AutoSleep. Best for: Apple Watch users who want deep/light sleep breakdowns. Highlights: Tracks automatically while you sleep with the watch on — no start/stop; quality score and bedtime tips.
- Sleep Cycle. Best for: People who sleep without a watch and want phone-based tracking. Highlights: Uses the mic to analyze movement and snoring; smart alarm in light sleep so waking up hurts less.
- Mi Fitness. Best for: Xiaomi Band/watch users. Highlights: Free and tightly integrated with Xiaomi bands; heart rate, SpO2, and sleep in one; great value.

##### 3. Meditation and relaxation

- Calm. Best for: Stressed professionals and people who struggle to fall asleep. Highlights: "Daily Calm" guided meditations + Sleep Stories (celebrity narrators); beautiful interface; rich English content.
- Tide. Best for: Chinese-speaking users who need white noise to focus or sleep. Highlights: Polished domestic app — nature sounds + meditation courses + Pomodoro; friendly Chinese guidance.
- Little Sleep. Best for: People who need sleep sounds and want to record sleep talk/snoring. Highlights: Stackable white noise (rain + fan + campfire), sleep timer; sleep-talk recordings that are fun and useful.

##### 4. Women's health

- Flo. Best for: Cycle tracking, trying to conceive, and women's health. Highlights: Accurate AI predictions for periods/ovulation; anonymous community Q&A; polished design.
- Meiyou. Best for: Users in China who want Chinese community content and education. Highlights: Rich local content tailored by stage — period/TTC/pregnancy/parenting.

##### 5. Medication reminders

- Medication Assistant. Best for: People on long-term meds and adult children managing parents' medicine. Highlights: Scan to ID drugs, auto-build a med list, pop-up reminders, share adherence logs with family.

##### 6. Data hubs

- Apple Health / Google Fit. Best for: All smartphone users. Highlights: Aggregates data from your phone, band, and third-party health apps (steps, heart rate, sleep, cycles) into one "health record center."

On the road, a few good apps save you from countless headaches. Picks by stage of the trip below.

##### 1. Flights and trains

- 12306. Best for: Anyone taking trains in China. Highlights: The only official ticket channel; waitlist purchases (auto-buys when someone refunds); easy student/group tickets.
- VariFlight. Best for: People sensitive to delays and those doing airport pickups. Highlights: Accurate delay predictions; share your trip so family can watch your location and ETA.

- Amap (Gaode). Best for: City users in China, drivers and ride-hailing. Highlights: Accurate live traffic, great at avoiding jams; ride-hailing one-stop; rich transit transfer options.
- Baidu Maps. Best for: People who need detailed POIs and indoor navigation. Highlights: Street View/AR navigation; detailed indoor maps for malls and attractions; fun voice packs.
- Google Maps. Best for: International travelers. Highlights: Best global coverage, honest local reviews; Street View and offline maps are lifesavers abroad.

##### 3. Translation

- Google Translate. Best for: Travelers and cross-language learners. Highlights: "Conversation" real-time two-way translation and camera instant lookup — travel essentials; offline language packs supported.

##### 4. Currency conversion

- Minimalist Currency. Best for: Travel, overseas shopping, cross-border buys. Highlights: Ultra-clean interface, 160+ currencies, live rates, multi-currency compare; long-press a number to convert quickly.

##### 5. Weather

- System Weather / China Weather. Best for: Everyone — the first stop for forecasts and official alerts. Highlights: OEM weather apps and official channels like "China Weather" push rainstorm, typhoon, and heat alerts as banners/notifications. Authoritative.
- Caiyun Weather. Best for: People who need minute-level rain forecasts. Highlights: Industry-leading "will it rain in the next two hours" accuracy; animated rain map; proactive alerts before rain starts.
- Carrot Weather. Best for: Weather fans who want fun and rich widgets. Highlights: Snarky AI voice reports, tons of custom widgets, pro radar. Paid version unlocks more.
- Alert essentials: Weather apps need notification permission to deliver alerts. Alerts come in blue, yellow, orange, and red; red means the highest risk of severe weather — adjust your plans. See Section 7 of this chapter for more setup tips ("Weather alerts / disaster alerts / earthquake alerts").

##### 6. Travel guides

- Mafengwo. Best for: Detailed planners, road trips, deep travel. Highlights: Rich user trip reports; thorough destination tips/routes/what to avoid; downloadable offline guides.
- Xiaohongshu (RED). Best for: Young travelers hunting real experiences, hidden spots, and food. Highlights: Visual posts and short videos; fast-updating "3 days in XX city" guides; great search.

#### 4. Automation for beginners

Automation is leverage: configure once, save effort for a long time — compressing dozens of daily taps to zero. This section is just a concept intro; see Chapter 23, "Computational Thinking and Automation," for details.

- iOS Shortcuts: Apple's official automation tool. No coding needed — chain actions like "when I get home: turn on Wi-Fi + dim the screen + read the weather" with one tap.
- Android automation: Built-in scene tools (like Xiaomi "Automations" or Huawei "Smart Scene") are the easiest to start with. Tasker is powerful but has a steep learning curve — better for tinkerers.
- Core idea: First spot the things you repeat every day, then ask "can one trigger + several actions replace this?" For detailed examples and advanced tricks, see Chapter 23.

#### 5. Browsers and search engines: Your gateway to information

A browser is your window to the internet; a search engine is your compass in the sea of information. Use them well and you'll avoid a lot of detours.

##### How a browser works:

When you type a web address, the browser: ① asks a DNS server for the IP behind the domain; ② sends an HTTP/HTTPS request to that server; ③ the server returns page code (HTML, CSS, JavaScript); ④ the browser parses and renders it into the page you see.

##### Recommended browsers:

- Microsoft Edge: Chromium-based, fast and compatible. Built-in Copilot AI can summarize pages, translate, and chat. Cross-device sync. Strong personal recommendation as a main browser.
- Safari: Apple's default browser, deeply integrated and power-efficient. Good privacy, but fewer extensions.
- Chrome: Most widely used worldwide, with a huge extension library. But it's memory-hungry and drains phone batteries faster.

##### Choosing a search engine:

- Bing: Microsoft's search engine, tightly integrated with Edge. AI summaries give answers, not just links — very efficient. Image search is solid too.
- Yandex: Russia's largest search engine, strong at image search and reverse image search. Great for finding similar images or identifying objects.
- Baidu: The main option for searching in China, but it shows a lot of ads — you need a critical eye.
- Google: The most precise search engine globally, but it needs special network access inside China.

##### Guide to efficient research:

- Use AI search: Bing's AI summaries and Edge's Copilot can answer questions directly and pull key facts out of huge result lists — much faster than clicking link after link.
- Spot ads and false info: Results marked "Ad" or "Sponsored" are paid placements, not organic. Check the source's authority and prefer official sites and known media.
- Download apps safely: Search "app name + official site," like "WeChat official site," and click results with the official domain. Avoid third-party download sites — they often bundle junkware.
- Spotting the real official site: For Windows 11 go to microsoft.com; for Chrome go to google.com. Matching the domain to the brand is the most basic check.

##### Website domain basics:

A domain is a site's "street address." Reading it helps you judge what a site is and where it comes from:

- Common suffixes:
- .cn — China's country domain
- .org — Nonprofit organizations
- .net — Network service providers
- .edu — Educational institutions
- .gov — Government agencies
- .xyz/.tech/.info — Newer generic domains, cheap to get, use caution

- Country/region domains:
- .kr — Korea
- .uk — United Kingdom
- .de — Germany
- .tw — Taiwan
- .hk — Hong Kong
- Safety tip: .gov and .edu sites are usually more trustworthy because registration is strict. Cheap domains like .xyz and .top are easy for phishing sites to grab — stay alert.

##### Ad blocking:

- AdGuard. Best for: Anyone annoyed by pop-ups, banner ads, and in-app ads. Highlights: System-wide ad blocking (not just the browser), filters in-app banners and splash tracking, saves data, speeds up pages. Paid version covers all devices.

##### Privacy browsers:

- Brave. Best for: Everyday browsers who care about privacy and hate ad tracking. Highlights: Chromium-based, blocks ads and trackers out of the box, built-in Tor private windows, faster and lighter than Chrome.
- DuckDuckGo. Best for: People who don't want one search to follow them everywhere with ads. Highlights: Built-in search engine (no search history), one-tap "Fire Button" clears all traces, consistent cross-platform experience.

#### 6. Useful system tools: Cleanup, spam blocking, and safety

Beyond productivity and health apps, daily life often comes down to three things: fewer interruptions, enough storage, and keeping scammers out. This section first covers handy utilities, then zooms in on built-in phone spam blocking — many people own a phone for years and never flip these switches on.

##### 1. System cleanup and storage

- Qingzhuo. Best for: Android users who want a lightweight way to clear cache, leftover files, and empty folders without a bloated "phone manager" suite. Highlights: Tiny, no ads; deep scan for leftovers, large-file finder, storage breakdown. More restrained than commercial "one-tap boosters" — good for hands-on users doing regular maintenance. Note: Check paths before cleaning. Don't blindly select WeChat, gallery, or document folders. Prefer the brand's built-in storage cleaner for system caches; treat third-party tools as supplements.
- Built-in storage cleaner. Best for: Everyone, especially older users and people who want zero hassle. Highlights: Android [Settings] > [Storage] > [Clean up]; iOS [Settings] > [General] > [iPhone Storage]. The system knows its own app cache structure best and is least likely to delete something important. Make this your first choice.
- WeChat/QQ built-in storage tools. Best for: Heavy chatters. Highlights: WeChat [Me] > [Settings] > [General] > [Storage] — clean by chat or file type. Safer than outside "WeChat cleaner" apps.
- Rule of thumb: If the system or app has a built-in option, use that instead of a third party. If you can select by path or type, avoid "clean everything." The goal is freeing space — not wiping important data along with it.

##### 2. Anti-spam and safety utilities

- National Anti-Fraud Center. Best for: Everyone — especially parents, finance-related jobs, and people who shop online or take unknown calls often. Highlights: Official app; call warnings, SMS risk tips, suspicious URL checks. Linked to police anti-fraud databases — the most official layer of the "three lines of defense."
- Brand security centers / phone managers. Best for: Users of that brand. Highlights: Huawei Phone Manager, Xiaomi Security Center, OPPO Phone Manager, vivo Phone Manager, and so on. Bundle spam blocking, virus scans, permission management, and payment protection. Usually free and better integrated with the system — don't install a second commercial security suite on top and cause permission clashes.
- Carrier spam blocking. Best for: People bombed by robocalls and AI voice calls. Highlights: China Mobile: text KTFSR to 10086. China Telecom: follow "Tianyi Anti-Harassment." China Unicom: set up in "Unicom Mobile Manager." Blocking happens on the carrier side, so calls never ring — a batch of spam numbers gets stopped before they reach you.
- AdGuard. Best for: Anyone annoyed by pop-ups, banner ads, and in-app ads. Highlights: System-wide ad blocking (not just the browser), filters in-app banners and splash tracking, saves data, speeds up pages. Paid version covers all devices.
- Be careful with "cleaner / spam blocker" all-in-one suites: Some third-party managers sit in the background and keep asking for contacts and SMS permissions. Their blocking may be no better than the system's, and they burn battery and raise privacy risks. Prefer built-in features first, then the official anti-fraud app.

#### 7. Weather alerts, disaster warnings, and earthquake early warnings

Spam blocking stops phone scams; early warnings stop natural disasters and extreme weather. Many people install a weather app just for the temperature, but never turn on notifications or earthquake warnings. In the seconds to tens of seconds before a red rainstorm alert, typhoon, or quake, your phone could buy you precious time to get to safety.

##### 1. Weather alerts: Rainstorms, typhoons, heat

- Official channels first: Alerts from the China Meteorological Administration, National Meteorological Center, and provincial bureaus are the most authoritative. Follow the "China Weather" WeChat account and Weibo, or install official apps like "China Weather."
- System weather apps: Huawei Weather, Mi Weather, OPPO Weather, vivo Weather, Apple Weather, and others usually push local observatory alerts. Open the weather app settings and make sure "Severe weather alerts" notifications are on.
- Minute-level rain apps like Caiyun Weather: Handy before commuting or school pickup to decide "umbrella or not in the next 30 minutes." For official warnings, still rely on the meteorological authority.
- Alert colors (general):
- Yellow: Be extra careful; cut unnecessary outdoor activity.
- Orange: Serious. Stay indoors if you can and keep away from danger zones.
- Red: Extremely serious. Follow closures and evacuation guidance. Don't push through flooded roads, flash-flood areas, or high-wind zones.

- Common alert types: rainstorm, blizzard, typhoon, heat wave, cold snap, gale, lightning, hail, icy roads, fog, drought, geological hazard risk.

##### 2. Natural disaster warnings: Not just "rain"

- Who issues them: Weather bureaus handle meteorological alerts. Geological hazards and flash floods are often issued jointly by natural resources, water resources, and emergency management departments. Local governments also push via emergency radio, SMS, and 12379.
- Get warnings on multiple channels:
- Follow local "Emergency Management" and "Weather Bureau" accounts on WeChat.
- 12379 national alert channels (SMS/services, depending on your region).
- TV, emergency radio, village/community loudspeakers — in mountains or flood season, don't rely on your phone alone.

- Outdoors and travel tips: In a typhoon, stay away from billboards and trees. In heavy rain, avoid underground garages, tunnels, and underpasses. In mountain areas, if a "geological hazard weather risk" alert is issued, leave steep slopes and valleys immediately.
- Pair with the emergency handbook: For post-disaster contact loss, dead phones, or location-based rescue calls, see Chapter 32 (earthquake/disaster scenarios) and Chapter 7 (satellite messaging without a network).

##### 3. Earthquake early warning: Not the same as "earthquake prediction"

Earthquake Early Warning (EEW) pushes an alert seconds to tens of seconds before damaging waves (especially S-waves) reach you. It uses the fact that radio waves beat seismic waves and stations detect P-waves first. It cannot predict whether a quake will happen, but it can buy time to "take cover nearby, cut fire sources, get out of elevators." Closer to the epicenter, the warning window shrinks — sometimes to nothing.

- Official and industry options: In mainland China, common sources include capabilities tied to the China Earthquake Networks Center and services promoted by groups like the Institute of Care-Life (Chengdu). Some phones ship with or can receive these warnings online.
- Third-party apps: Search the app store for "earthquake warning" apps from official or research bodies. Check the developer before installing — avoid fake "quake predictor" apps that demand a pile of unrelated permissions.
- What to do when an alert sounds: Indoors, take cover nearby (under a table, inside corners of load-bearing walls, next to sturdy furniture) and protect your head and neck. Stay away from windows, exterior walls, and tall furniture. Don't blindly rush the stairs or jump. If you're in a lift, get off at the nearest floor. Outdoors, move to open ground away from power lines, billboards, and unstable buildings. After the shaking, check gas and wiring and watch for aftershocks.
- Don't silence it: Earthquake alerts should be allowed to break through silent/Do Not Disturb. Keep your phone within reach at night, and don't set the ringer to zero.

#### 8. Cross-device teamwork: Remote control, fast transfer, account safety

A phone feels smooth when it teams up with your computer and tablet. This section adds high-frequency tools: remote-control your PC from a phone, AI office work and remote collaboration, live wallpapers, LAN file transfer, cross-device clipboard, authenticator 2FA, and linking paper scanning with schedules. For systematic multi-device setups, see Chapter 17.

##### 1. Control your computer from a phone: Sunlogin / ToDesk

- Sunlogin Remote Control. Best for: People who need to operate a home/office Windows or Mac while out — also popular for helping parents fix phones remotely (see Chapter 27). Highlights: Mature domestic ecosystem; phone app controls the desktop, transfers files, and can power on/off (some features need hardware). Free tier is enough for light personal use.
- ToDesk. Best for: People who want stable, low-latency remote work/ops. Highlights: Device ID + password connection; phone controls PC (and sometimes PC controls phone, depending on version); file transfer and multi-monitor switching feel smooth. Domestic nodes usually perform well.
- Basic setup: Install the client on the computer and sign in / stay online → install the app on the phone → connect with device ID + access password (or the same account) → use the phone as a trackpad/gestures for the desktop.
- Safety rules (memorize these):
- For personal use: Turn on two-step verification, change default passwords, sign out or disable unattended access when done. On work computers, follow company security policy.
- In public, remote work carefully — watch for people peeking at your screen and passwords.

##### 2. AI office apps: Trae / WorkBuddy / Dumate

- Trae. Best for: Coding and document collaboration, friendly in Chinese environments. Highlights: ByteDance's AI IDE/assistant; SOLO mode can spin up a project from one sentence. Efficient for daily coding and document edits. If it runs on a computer, you can pair it with remote control or official companion features on mobile to check/follow tasks (check the latest product form). See Chapter 41 for the AI coding tools landscape.
- WorkBuddy. Best for: Office users who want AI help with meeting notes, email, reports, and work docs. Highlights: An AI assistant aimed at office scenarios — great for draft → polish → summarize flows. Start some workflows on the phone; leave heavy lifting to the computer.
- Dumate. Best for: Teams/individuals who want multi-person collaboration with docs and knowledge management in one place. Highlights: AI collaboration office tool focused on gathering materials and co-editing. View and lightly edit on the phone; do complex layout on a bigger computer screen.
- What "phone remote-controls computer" means here: Many AI office features run on desktop/web. From your phone, connect back via Sunlogin/ToDesk or use the product's own mobile app/webpage to "remote" AI tasks on the computer during your commute. Rule: heavy compute and long docs on the computer; check progress and tweak a few lines on the phone.
- Privacy note: Before uploading contracts, ID cards, or customer data, read the product's privacy terms and company compliance rules. For work materials, prefer tools your organization specifies.

##### 3. Live wallpapers: miHoYo Desktop Mate (phone + PC)

- Desktop Mate. Best for: Fans of miHoYo characters who want free live wallpapers. Highlights: Free live wallpaper app from miHoYo on phone and PC. Character Live2D/animated performances can sit on your desktop; some devices/systems allow lock screen or home wallpaper use.
- Battery and performance: Live wallpapers use more power and GPU than static images. On low battery or older phones, use them only while charging, or lower the frame rate if the app offers it.
- Install source: Prefer the official site or a trusted app store. Avoid third-party "cracked" builds with bundled malware. On PC, watch the install path and whether auto-start is needed.

##### 4. Fast LAN file transfer: LocalSend / Feige / AirDrop

- LocalSend. Best for: Mixed Android/Windows/iOS/Mac/Linux setups that want cross-platform transfers without public cloud. Highlights: Open source, direct LAN connection. Send photos, videos, and docs on the same Wi-Fi — no cable, no vendor servers. Speed depends on your router.
- Feige (IPMsg). Best for: Office Windows PCs and older LAN office setups. Highlights: A classic LAN transfer/chat tool, good for big files on an intranet. Download from a trusted source; on company networks follow IT policy.
- AirDrop. Best for: iPhone, iPad, and Mac users. Highlights: One-tap sharing in the Apple ecosystem. Bluetooth discovery + Wi-Fi Direct, no mobile data. Set "Contacts Only" to reduce strangers pinging you.
- Also worth knowing: Mi Share, Huawei Share, vivo Share, and similar brand tools are very fast within the same brand. WeChat "File Transfer Helper" is convenient but limited by file size and signed-in devices. For big files, prefer LocalSend/AirDrop.
- Tips: Both devices on the same Wi-Fi. Public Wi-Fi is risky for sensitive files — prefer a personal hotspot. After transfer, confirm the file opens completely on the receiving side.

##### 5. Cross-device clipboard: KDE Connect / brand phone-PC link

- Apple Universal Clipboard. Best for: iPhone + Mac users. Highlights: Sign in with the same Apple ID and enable Handoff — copy on the phone, paste on the Mac (and vice versa). Works for text and some files.
- KDE Connect. Best for: Android + Windows/Linux/Mac users who want open-source clipboard sync, notification mirroring, and remote input. Highlights: Pair on the LAN to sync clipboards, push phone notifications to the PC, use the phone as a slide clicker/trackpad. Data doesn't go through third-party accounts.
- Brand phone-PC links: Huawei Multi-Screen Collaboration / Super Device, Mi Continuity, vivo Share, OPPO Connect, Honor Connect, and others often include cross-screen clipboard and drag-and-drop. Best experience within the same brand.
- Windows + Android official path: "Phone Link / Link to Windows" (evolved from Your Phone) lets you see phone notifications and photos on the PC and copy/paste in supported cases. Deeper on Samsung and some other models.
- Safety note: Clipboards can retain passwords and codes. On shared computers, be careful with global sync. After pasting into a password field, avoid a second paste that leaks it. Some keyboards also read the clipboard — check permissions.

##### 6. Authenticator 2FA: Aegis / Microsoft Authenticator

- Why an authenticator instead of SMS: SMS codes can be hijacked via SIM swap. TOTP codes are generated locally on a time algorithm, don't need phone signal, and resist phishing/interception better (still protect against malware and screenshots).
- Aegis Authenticator. Best for: Android users who prefer open source and local control. Highlights: Free and open source, encrypted backup and import/export, clean UI, unlock token list with fingerprint/face.
- Microsoft Authenticator. Best for: Microsoft account, Office 365, and work account users; works on iOS/Android. Highlights: Push approval for sign-ins, password monitoring, and account backup (depending on version). Tightly integrated with Windows and enterprise identity.
- Google Authenticator / 2FAS, etc.: Common in their ecosystems too. What matters is encrypted backup so you don't lose tokens when you switch phones.
- Must-do when switching phones: Transfer from the old device, or use the service's "backup codes / recovery key." Print backup codes and store them in two places — not just on the same phone.
- Password managers: Some password vaults can store TOTP too. Convenient, but risk is concentrated. For high-value accounts, split the job: "password manager + separate authenticator." See Chapter 14.
