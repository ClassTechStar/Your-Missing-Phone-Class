### Chapter 5: Make Your Phone Understand You Better

{class="chapter-intro mb-4"}
A few minutes of setup brings a huge boost to your experience. These settings are like "tuning" your phone to better match your usage habits.

#### I. Sound and Vibration

Set a ringtone and notification sound you like. Use silent mode (for meetings) and Do Not Disturb mode (for sleeping) flexibly—the world instantly quiets down.

- <strong>Ringtone</strong>: Pick one you can hear and isn't embarrassing.
- <strong>Vibration mode</strong>: Vibration alerts when on silent.
- <strong>Do Not Disturb mode</strong>: Can set exceptions, like allowing calls from contacts.
- <strong>Volume separation</strong>: Media volume, ringtone volume, and alarm volume can be adjusted separately.

#### II. Display and Brightness

- <strong>Auto brightness</strong>: Lets the phone adjust based on ambient light—worry-free.
- <strong>Eye comfort/Night Shift mode</strong>: Filters blue light, making phone use more comfortable at night.
- <strong>Dark mode</strong>: Saves power (especially effective on OLED screens) and isn't harsh on the eyes at night. Many apps also support dark mode.
- <strong>Screen refresh rate</strong>: Adjustable on some Android phones—higher (like 120Hz) means smoother scrolling.
- <strong>Auto-lock</strong>: Set to 15 or 30 seconds—both secure and power-saving.
- <strong>Font size</strong>: Think the text is too small? Make it bigger.

#### III. Wallpaper, Themes, and Home Screen

Change to a nice wallpaper (static or dynamic)—even your mood changes. Android brand phones often have theme stores that can change the entire set of icons and style.

<strong>Home screen widgets</strong>: Put weather, schedule, and music controls on the home screen for one-step access. Both iOS and Android support this—long-press an empty area on the home screen to add.

#### IV. Efficiency Settings: Make Your Phone Your "External Brain"

These are "once set, never go back" features—each one can significantly boost your daily efficiency.

##### 1. Focus / Do Not Disturb Mode: Reclaim Attention Stolen by Notifications

- <strong>Core idea</strong>: Not "look at your phone less," but "focus when you should focus, connect when you should connect."
- <strong>Usage suggestions</strong>:<br> · Work mode: Only allow "starred" contacts and essential apps through;<br> · Sleep mode: Auto-enable, only allow alarms;<br> · Fitness / Reading / Meetings: customize based on the current activity.
- <strong>Advanced usage</strong>: Use automation + location / time triggers to switch modes automatically. For example: iOS Shortcuts can set "arrive at company → auto-enter work mode."

##### 2. Notification Management: Stop Red Badges from "Relentlessly Pestering"

- <strong>Turn off marketing notifications</strong>: 90% of push notifications you don't actually need.
- <strong>Sort by importance</strong>: Pin interpersonal communications (phone, SMS, instant messaging) to the top; news, shopping, and social platform notifications can be set to "silent delivery."
- <strong>Group notifications</strong>: Fold similar notifications together to avoid being overwhelmed by dozens of messages with one unlock.

##### 3. Quick Gestures: Stay One Step Ahead

- <strong>Screenshot</strong>: Power + Volume Down (iOS full-screen) / Power + Volume Down (Android universal).
- <strong>Long screenshot / Scroll screenshot</strong>: After a regular screenshot, tap the "Long Screenshot" option.
- <strong>One-tap scan</strong>: Add a "Scan" icon to Control Center / Quick Settings.
- <strong>Back Tap (iPhone)</strong>: In [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Touch] &gt; [Back Tap], bind actions like "Screenshot" or "Lock Screen."
- <strong>Voice wake</strong>: Siri / Xiaoai / Xiaoyi / Xiaobu—just call out to check weather or set an alarm.

##### 4. Input Method: The Most Underestimated Efficiency Tool

- <strong>Choose the right input method</strong>:<br> · Sogou / Baidu: Large lexicon, fast trending word updates;<br> · iFlytek: Highest voice input recognition rate, a note-taking lifesaver in meetings;<br> · Gboard (Google): Clean and simple, strong cross-platform sync, supports gesture typing;<br> · iOS built-in: Best privacy, deeply integrated with the system.
- <strong>Essential toggles</strong>: Smart correction, word prediction, cloud lexicon sync, swipe up for numbers and symbols.
- <strong>Privacy tip</strong>: Use "full-sentence prediction" or "personalized recommendations" with caution—they may upload your input content.

##### 5. Home Screen and Multitasking: Smooth Out Your Workflow

- <strong>App split screen / Picture-in-Picture</strong>: Reply to messages while watching video, take notes while browsing.
- <strong>Small window / Floating window</strong>: WeChat / TikTok support "small window mode"—reply and dismiss.
- <strong>Pin frequently used apps</strong>: Only put the 4 most frequent apps in the Dock (bottom of screen) to reduce search time.

#### V. Accessibility: The Power of Tech for Good

Don't be intimidated by the name "accessibility"—these features are for everyone. They were originally designed for people with disabilities, but they're also useful for ordinary people—temporarily injured hands, holding a baby, broken earbuds on the subway, or needing reading glasses for small text... they can all save the day. Ultimately, accessible design embodies the philosophy of <strong>tech for good</strong>: letting everyone equally enjoy digital life.

##### 1. "Eyes" for Visually Impaired Users—Complete Screen Reader Guide

Screen readers are the core tool for visually impaired users to use phones. It reads <strong>all visible content on the screen aloud</strong>, and users operate the phone through gestures and voice. Below we introduce the usage of iOS "VoiceOver" and Android "TalkBack" respectively.

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
<i class="fas fa-volume-up mr-2"></i>iOS VoiceOver

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>How to enable</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [VoiceOver], turn on the switch. You can also triple-click the side button (or Home button) to quickly toggle.

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Basic gestures</strong>:

{class="list-decimal pl-6 mt-1 space-y-0.5 text-sm"}
1. One-finger swipe: Browse screen content element by element;
2. One-finger double-tap: Activate element;
3. :
4. Three-finger swipe: Switch pages;
5. One-finger triple-tap: Zoom or select (different from regular triple-tap).

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Personalization</strong>: [VoiceOver] &gt; [Speech], you can adjust <strong>speaking rate</strong>,<strong>pitch</strong>, and switch between different voice packs. iOS has built-in "Enhanced Speech" and "Speech Controller"—the former reduces breathing sounds and pauses for a more natural listening experience.
:::

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
<i class="fas fa-volume-up mr-2"></i>Android TalkBack

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>How to enable</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [TalkBack], turn on the switch. Or long-press both volume keys for 3 seconds to quickly enable (supported by some brands).

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Basic gestures</strong>:

{class="list-decimal pl-6 mt-1 space-y-0.5 text-sm"}
1. One-finger left/right swipe: Switch focus element;
2. One-finger double-tap: Activate element;
3. Two-finger up/down/left/right swipe: Scroll;
4. Three-finger single-tap: Enter reading mode (continuous reading);
5. Four-finger single-tap: Pause/resume reading.

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Personalization</strong>: [TalkBack] &gt; [Text-to-Speech Settings], you can adjust <strong>speech rate</strong>,<strong>pitch</strong>, and switch <strong>the speech engine</strong>(we recommend installing the Google Text-to-Speech engine for more natural reading).
:::

{class="font-semibold mt-4 mb-1"}
##### 2. Make Small Text Big, Make Blurry Clear—Visual Adjustments

##### Zoom Gestures

- <strong>iOS</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Zoom], after enabling,<strong>three-finger tap the screen</strong> to zoom in. Full-screen zoom is the default, with optional window zoom (a partial magnification pane).<strong>Pinch with two fingers to adjust magnification (1.5x to 15x)</strong>, three-finger double-tap to lock the current zoom ratio.
- <strong>Android</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Magnification], three-finger tap to zoom, three-finger tap again to exit; or directly <strong>three-finger triple-tap the screen</strong> to enable temporary magnification (up to 8x).

##### High Contrast Text / Bold Text

- <strong>iOS</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Display &amp; Text Size], turn on "Bold Text," "Increase Contrast," "Reduce Transparency," etc. "Smart Invert" can keep text black on light backgrounds, avoiding white-on-white.
- <strong>Android</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Display] &gt; [Font Size], with four levels: "Small," "Default," "Large," and "Extra Large." Huawei/Xiaomi additionally provide "Bold" and "High Contrast Text" switches.

##### Color Filters (Color Blind-Friendly)

- <strong>iOS</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Display &amp; Text Size] &gt; [Color Filters], provides <strong>four color blind modes: red/green/blue/yellow</strong>, plus "Grayscale" and "Invert Colors" options. Take the built-in color vision test once before choosing a filter.
- <strong>Android</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Color Correction], includes <strong>three color blind modes</strong> plus an "Invert Colors" switch.

{class="font-semibold mt-4 mb-1"}
##### 3. AI Image Description: Let AI Be Your "Eyes"

When a screen reader encounters an image, the traditional approach is to skip it or just read "image." But now AI can <strong>describe image content in a sentence</strong>, which is very helpful for recognizing CAPTCHAs, reading images, and identifying menus.

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
<i class="fas fa-robot mr-2"></i>AI Image Description Support by System

{class="list-disc pl-6 mt-1 space-y-1 text-sm"}
- <strong>iOS 15+</strong>: Enable "Image Descriptions" in [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [VoiceOver] &gt; [Speech Descriptions]. The system calls a <strong>local AI model</strong> to automatically generate text descriptions of images (requires iPhone 12 or later).
- <strong>Newer TalkBack app</strong>: Google TalkBack integrates <strong>image Q&amp; A</strong> in newer versions—you can ask "What's in this image?" and AI answers in real time (not strictly dependent on Android system version—just update the TalkBack app to experience it).
- <strong>Third-party apps</strong>: "Seeing AI" (iOS), "Lookout" (Android), "Xiaoyi Bangbang" (Huawei) are all excellent AI visual assistants that can recognize objects, read documents, and identify facial expressions.
:::

::: div .practice-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
<i class="fas fa-lightbulb mr-2"></i>Use Cases

{class="mt-1"}
Try asking about a street photo, "How many people are in this image? What are they doing?", or open a food delivery app and let AI read out the dish names in the menu image—<strong>this is true "tech making life more equal"</strong>.
:::

##### 4. "Ears" for Hearing-Impaired Users—Complete Live Captions Guide

Live Captions is a <strong>system-level speech-to-text</strong> feature—especially useful for hearing-impaired users in noisy environments, libraries, or when communicating in a foreign language.

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
<i class="fas fa-closed-captioning mr-2"></i>Enabling and Optimizing Live Captions

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>iOS</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Live Captions], turn on the switch. The system detects all device audio (calls, videos, podcasts, FaceTime, voice memos) and automatically displays captions at the top of the screen.

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Android 12+</strong>: Press the <strong>volume keys</strong> → tap the "Live Caption" icon in the popup; or enable in [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Live Caption].

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Accuracy optimization tips</strong>:

{class="list-decimal pl-6 mt-1 space-y-0.5 text-sm"}
1. Use in a <strong>quiet environment</strong> as much as possible, away from air conditioners and machinery noise;
2. The speaker <strong>articulates clearly and speaks at a moderate pace</strong> for the highest recognition accuracy;
3. Multi-person conversations reduce accuracy—use "show only one person" mode;
4. Supports mainstream languages like Mandarin Chinese, Cantonese, and English;<strong>minority languages have lower recognition rates</strong>;
5. Background music or voice interference during speech will cause intermittent recognition.
:::

##### 5. Dual-Channel Call Scenarios: Speech-to-Text + Text-to-Speech

When users <strong>can neither hear nor speak</strong>(such as sudden voice loss, laryngeal surgery recovery, severe stuttering), the phone can still act as a "spokesperson."

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
<i class="fas fa-phone mr-2"></i>Real-Time Call Transcription (TTY / RTT)

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>iOS</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [RTT/TTY], after enabling, a keyboard appears on screen during calls—the other party's speech is transcribed to text in real time, and <strong>your typed text is also read aloud</strong> to them.

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Android</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Live Transcribe] + [Sound Notifications]. "Live Transcribe" displays the other party's speech as text in real time; "Sound Notifications" converts your input to speech when voice isn't convenient.

{class="mt-1"}
<strong>Compatibility</strong>:

{class="list-disc pl-6 mt-1 space-y-1 text-sm"}
- <strong>RTT (Real-Time Text)</strong>: Both parties need support; text appears as you type with no delay; mainstream carriers now support it;
- <strong>TTY (Teletypewriter)</strong>: Requires a dedicated TTY device; it is gradually being replaced by RTT;
- WeChat/QQ <strong>voice-to-text feature</strong> is also an alternative: long-press a voice message → "Convert to Text."
:::

##### 6. "Small but Practical" Features for Everyone

- <strong>Back Tap</strong>(iOS 14+): [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Touch] &gt; [Back Tap], can bind <strong>double-tap/triple-tap</strong> to trigger screenshot, lock screen, control center, etc. Especially handy when carrying packages with both hands.
- <strong>Assistive Touch / Floating Ball</strong>: iOS [Accessibility] &gt; [Touch] &gt; [Assistive Touch], Android [Accessibility] &gt; [Floating Navigation]. Virtual buttons replace gestures or physical buttons—lifesaver when buttons are unresponsive or for one-handed use.
- <strong>Switch Control</strong>: Control the phone through external switches, single clicks, or head movements—<strong>designed for users with physical disabilities</strong>. iOS [Accessibility] &gt; [Switch Control], Android [Accessibility] &gt; [Switch Access].
- <strong>Sound Recognition</strong>(iOS): [Accessibility] &gt; [Sound Recognition], after enabling, the phone "listens" for doorbells, baby cries, fire alarms, sirens, etc., and <strong>upon recognition, vibrates + sends a screen notification</strong>—very valuable for hearing-impaired users and during sleep.
- <strong>LED Flash Alerts</strong>: iOS [Accessibility] &gt; [Audio/Visual] &gt; [LED Flash for Alerts], Android [Settings] &gt; [Notifications] &gt; [Flash Alerts]. The flash blinks for incoming calls and notifications—<strong>sensible even without sound in noisy environments</strong>.
- <strong>Mono Audio</strong>: [Settings] &gt; [Accessibility] &gt; [Audio] &gt; [Mono Audio], merges left and right channels. Users with single-ear hearing loss won't lose half the audio information.
