### Chapter 8: Communication — Texts, Calls, and Social

{class="chapter-intro mb-4"}
Communication is the phone's original and core mission. Today it has grown far beyond simple calls and texts into a whole ecosystem of instant messaging, social networks, and email.

#### 1. Advanced features of the Phone app

The Phone app on modern phones is way more than a dialer. It packs in call recording, spam blocking, smart caller ID, and other powerful tools.

##### Call recording (mind the legal fine print):

- What it's for: Save important calls for work notes, interviews, customer commitments, or evidence in a dispute.
- Legal note: In mainland China, a call recording is generally admissible as evidence only with the other party's consent, or when it protects your own lawful rights.
- One-sided vs. two-sided consent: Legally, a recording made "without the other party's consent" is not automatically invalid. Under relevant Supreme People's Court interpretations, it can still be submitted as evidence as long as the method of recording did not seriously infringe others' lawful rights, violate prohibitive legal rules, or go against public order and good morals. That said, some provincial courts apply stricter standards for "secretly recorded" audio in civil cases; criminal cases usually face even stricter legality review.
- Regional differences: Courts in places like Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangdong are relatively lenient about one-party recordings as civil evidence. Recordings involving trade secrets or personal privacy need extra care anywhere — they may be excluded for infringing privacy rights.
- How to do it: Android usually shows a clear "Record" button on the call screen. iOS has no built-in call recording, so you'll need a third-party app or a Bluetooth headset with a recording feature.

::: div .warning-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
Hard rules for using recordings

{class="list-disc pl-6 mt-1 space-y-1"}
- Never use recordings for extortion, spreading them around, or publicly insulting others.
- Do not casually share content that involves third-party privacy (for example, accidentally catching someone else's conversation).
- In important situations, say out loud at the start of the call that "this call will be recorded." That keeps things compliant and makes the recording stronger as evidence.
:::

##### A closer look at spam-call blocking:

- How it works: The Phone app checks a huge online number database and shows "spam call," "scam call," or a company name when the phone rings.
- How to turn it on: On Android, open the Phone app > Settings > Spam blocking. On iOS, go to Settings > Phone > Call Blocking & Identification. For brand-by-brand switch paths and recommended options, see the comparison table in Chapter 13, "Useful System Tools."
- Main labeling platforms: In China, number labeling is mainly handled by three data providers — Tencent Mobile Manager, 360 Mobile Security, and Dianhuabang (Tapir). Carrier features like "caller name" display and "spam blocking" often pull from the same data. Phone makers (Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo) mostly partner with these providers for their label databases.
- What to do if you're mislabeled: If your legitimate work number is wrongly tagged as "food delivery," "courier," or "spam," you can appeal through these channels:
- Dianhuabang (电话邦): visit the number-appeal platform haoma.tk.cn and submit identity verification.
- 360: Submit via the "Number appeal" feature inside the 360 Mobile Security app.
- Tencent: Submit on the "Number appeal" page in the "Tencent Mobile Manager" mini program.
- The big three carriers: China Mobile users can text "KTFSR" to 10086 to enable spam protection. China Telecom users can follow the "Tianyi Anti-Harassment" official account. China Unicom users can set it up in "Unicom Mobile Manager."

- Next-level spam blocking: Turn on your carrier's "high-frequency call blocking" (such as China Mobile's high-frequency call limit) to automatically block AI robocalls.
- Common built-in phone blocking features:
- Blacklist / whitelist: Manually block known spam numbers. Add contacts and courier/delivery numbers to the whitelist so they don't get caught by mistake.
- SMS keyword filtering: Messages containing words like "prize," "loan," or "click this link" are automatically sent to the blocked box.
- iOS Silence Unknown Callers: Calls from numbers not in your contacts go quietly to voicemail without interrupting you. Handy if you get lots of spam — just watch out for important calls from new numbers.
- Do Not Disturb integration: At night or during meetings, allow only contacts through, on a schedule. Your sleep quality will thank you.
- How apps work together: Prefer the phone's built-in blocking first, then layer on the National Anti-Fraud Center app and carrier spam protection. For cleanup tools, lightweight options like Qingzhuo are fine, but one security/cleanup suite is enough — see Chapter 13.

##### What VoLTE/VoNR are and how to turn them on:

- VoLTE (Voice over LTE): Voice calls over the 4G network. No more falling back to 2G/3G mid-call, so you can talk and browse at the same time, with clearer audio and faster call setup.
- VoNR (Voice over NR): Voice over 5G — the next step after VoLTE. Lower latency, better sound, and support for HD video calls. You need a 5G phone, a 5G plan, and local 5G coverage.
- How to enable: On iOS, go to Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data Options > Voice & Data and turn on "VoLTE." On Android, find the "VoLTE calls" switch under Settings > Mobile network > Mobile data > Advanced. Carriers usually turn it on by default. If your data drops during calls, VoLTE is probably off.

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
Quick tip

{class="mt-1"}
If your data suddenly slows or drops during a call, VoLTE/VoNR is probably off and you've fallen back to 2G/3G. Turn it on and you can "talk and scroll" at the same time.
:::

#### 2. SMS and iMessage/RCS

Traditional SMS isn't dead. It's still the reliable channel for verification codes, bank alerts, and official notices.

- iMessage: Messages sent over the internet between Apple devices, shown as blue bubbles. Supports high-quality photos and videos, read receipts, and more — free and end-to-end encrypted.
- RCS: A next-generation messaging protocol pushed by carriers worldwide and by Google. Features similar to iMessage or WeChat, but based on phone numbers.
- iMessage and RCS interoperability: Starting with iOS 18, Apple supports the RCS standard. That means iPhones and Android phones can exchange HD photos, read receipts, and typing indicators — goodbye "green bubble" gap. Both carriers need to support RCS, and both phones need a new enough OS.

##### RCS rollout in China:

- China Mobile: An early RCS adopter. Some provinces already support "5G Messages" with rich media and interactive cards. Needs a supported handset and activation at a store.
- China Telecom: Offers "Telecom 5G Messages." Some plans include it by default; older accounts may need to send a specific SMS command to activate.
- China Unicom: Rolling out 5G Messages in major cities, but device support is uneven. Many Android models still need "Enhanced messages" or "5G Messages" switched on manually in the Messages app.
- Real-world experience: RCS has rolled out more slowly than expected in China, mainly because WeChat dominates and device support is inconsistent. Day-to-day chat still runs on WeChat; RCS mostly handles "official push" messages such as bank alerts and government notices.

::: div .warning-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
Verification-code safety rules

{class="list-disc pl-6 mt-1 space-y-1"}
- Never forward codes: Anyone who asks you to "read out a code" or "forward a text" may be a scammer — including people claiming to be "support," "couriers," or "police, prosecutors, or courts." Your verification code = your money.
- Phishing SMS: Be wary of links in texts about "ETC expired," "health card frozen," or "lost package compensation." Legitimate organizations never ask for card numbers or passwords via SMS links.
- Spotting the sender: Official bank texts usually come from short codes starting with 95 or from 1069 + a company signature. A "bank notice" from a normal 11-digit mobile number is almost always a scam.
- Two-factor backups: Don't casually change the phone number tied to important accounts. If you must, unlink all key accounts first — otherwise the new owner of your old number could reset your passwords via SMS codes.
:::

#### 3. WeChat/QQ: Managing China's national super-apps

WeChat and QQ have outgrown simple messaging and become "super-apps" for social, payments, and information.

##### Backing up and migrating chat history:

1. Log into WeChat on your PC or Mac.
2. Click the menu at the bottom left > [Migrate & Backup] > [Backup & Restore].
3. Choose "Back up chat history to computer," then pick the chats on your phone.
4. Migrating to a new phone: On the old phone, open WeChat [Me] > [Settings] > [General] > [Chat Log Migration & Backup] > [Migrate] > [Migrate to Phone/Tablet], then scan the QR code.

##### Deep cleaning storage space:

- Standard path: WeChat [Me] > [Settings] > [General] > [Storage] — clear cache and clean chat history by time or file type.
- Clean inside a chat: Open a chat > "..." at the top right > [Chat history] > [Manage storage] — delete photos, videos, and files in that chat while keeping the text.
- Original photos/videos: HD originals from group chats take the most space. In [Manage storage], select "Originals" to delete them in bulk.
- Turn off auto-download: [Settings] > [General] > [Photos, Videos, Files & Calls] > turn off "Auto-download" so group files don't silently pile up on Wi-Fi.
- Mini program cache: In [Discover] > [Mini Programs], long-press unused ones to delete and free their local cache.
- Compressed chat backups: When backing up on desktop WeChat, check "Compress" to shrink multi-GB chat histories to 30–50% of their size.

##### Group management tips:

- Group announcements: Owners/admins post them via "..." at the top right > [Group Announcement]. Everyone gets a strong notification — great for important notices. New members see it automatically.
- Group to-dos: The owner can turn a pinned message into a "Group To-do." A red badge appears above members' heads and clears when they tap done. Perfect for signups and task collection.
- Group chains (sign-up lists): Type "#chain" in the input box to start one — great for ordering food or tallying signups. Results auto-summarize at the top without flooding the chat.
- Group live: "+" at the top right > [Group Live] for a live stream only this group can watch. Good for company training, family gatherings, and product beta tests.
- Mute a group: Swipe a group name left in the list > [Mute] to tuck unimportant groups into "Muted groups," notifying you only when @mentioned.
- Only owners/admins can @everyone: In groups of 500+, regular members can't @everyone — less noise for everyone.

##### Fine-grained Moments privacy:

- Group visibility: When posting, tap "Who can see" and choose "Partially visible" or "Hide from," using tag groups you set up earlier (e.g., "Colleagues," "Family," "Clients").
- How far back friends can look: [Me] > [Settings] > [Friends' Permissions] > [Moments] > [Allow friends to view Moments] — choose "Last 3 days / 1 month / 6 months / All." "6 months" balances privacy and social life nicely.
- Hide someone's Moments: Long-press a post > [Hide their Moments] or [Unfollow] — they won't know.
- Moments ads: [Me] > [Settings] > [About WeChat] > [Personal Info & Permissions] > [Personalized Ads Management] to turn off behavior-based recommendations.
- Strangers see ten posts: [Friends' Permissions] > [Moments] > [Allow strangers to view 10 Moments] — turn this off so strangers can't scroll your history.

#### 4. Video call comparison

Each video call tool has its strengths. Picking the right one for the situation makes conversations smoother.

- WeChat video: Ubiquitous in China, easy for all ages. Great for everyday chats with family and friends. Supports up to 20 people (via the "Group Video" mini program). Downside: quality drops noticeably on poor networks.
- FaceTime: Apple-only, excellent quality, end-to-end encrypted. Since iOS 15, you can invite Android/Web users via a link, but the host must be on an Apple device. Great for cross-device family calls.
- Zoom: The go-to for international meetings. Free 40-minute meetings for up to 100 people. Screen sharing, virtual backgrounds, breakout rooms — solid for remote and cross-border work.
- Tencent Meeting: The office standard in China, deeply integrated with WeChat (scan to join). Up to 300 participants (paid). Cloud recording, live captions, meeting minutes — great for company training and large meetings.
- Which to pick: Everyday 1-on-1 → WeChat. Apple-to-Apple → FaceTime. Formal business → Zoom or Tencent Meeting (Tencent in China, Zoom across borders).

##### Bandwidth and data use reference:

- WeChat video calls: About 500 Kbps–1 Mbps; roughly 3–5 MB per minute at standard quality, 8–12 MB at HD.
- FaceTime: About 1 Mbps at SD, 2 Mbps for 720p, and 4+ Mbps for 1080p. Roughly 5–15 MB per minute.
- Zoom/Tencent Meeting: About 1.5–3 Mbps for 720p, 3–5 Mbps for 1080p. Roughly 300–800 MB per hour; hosts of multi-person meetings use more.
- Tip: Use Wi-Fi for long video calls. On mobile data, lower the quality yourself (turn off "HD video" in app settings) to save data.

##### Video call quality tips:

- Lighting: Face a light source with a window behind it (natural light is best). Avoid backlighting that turns your face into a silhouette.
- Angle: Keep the camera slightly above eye level to avoid a double chin. In landscape mode, hold the phone with both hands for stability.
- Audio: A headset prevents echo and howling. In a quiet room, don't use speakerphone.
- Network: Prefer Wi-Fi. On 4G/5G, stay near a window for a stronger signal. In group meetings, turn off other videos and keep only the speaker.
- Background blur: Tencent Meeting and Zoom both support AI blur to hide messy backgrounds. Wear solid colors so you don't "green-screen" into the background.

{class="font-semibold"}
Quick tip

{class="mt-1"}
When the connection lags, turn off video and keep audio before hanging up — more polite than dropping the call. Before important meetings, run Tencent Meeting's built-in "Network Test."

#### 5. Email: Set up and use it on your phone

- Add an email account: On iOS, Settings > Mail > Accounts > Add Account. On Android, there's usually a built-in "Email" app.
- Tips: Set different notifications for different accounts. Use swipe gestures to quickly archive, delete, or flag mail.

##### IMAP vs POP3 vs Exchange:

- POP3: Downloads mail and deletes the server copy by default. Good for a single device that needs offline reading. Downside: no history on new devices. No longer recommended.
- IMAP: Mail stays on the server; local is just a cache. Phones/PCs/tablets stay in sync — read on one, read everywhere. Today's mainstream recommendation.
- Exchange (including ActiveSync): Microsoft's protocol. Syncs calendar, contacts, and tasks as well as mail. First choice for work email. Supported by Outlook, NetEase Enterprise Mail, and Tencent Enterprise Mail.
- What to choose: Personal email → IMAP. Work email → Exchange first, IMAP second. Avoid POP3 unless you have a special archiving need.

##### Work email setup:

1. iOS: [Settings] > [Mail] > [Accounts] > [Add Account] > choose "Microsoft Exchange" or "Other."
2. Android: In the built-in "Email" app or Outlook, choose "Add account" and enter your full address and password.
3. Server settings (Exchange example): Server is often "mail.yourcompany.com" or "outlook.office365.com"; domain is your company short name.
4. If you see "Cannot verify server," enter settings manually: Incoming server (IMAP) port 993 with SSL; outgoing server (SMTP) port 465 or 587 with TLS.
5. After signing in, turn on "Push" in [Account Settings] for real-time mail, and turn off message preview to protect confidential content.

::: div .info-box .rounded-lg .p-4 .mt-3
{class="font-semibold"}
Heads-up

{class="mt-1"}
Work email usually needs an admin-issued "client authorization password" or "IMAP/SMTP auth code" — not your web login password. If login fails, generate a code in webmail settings first, then enter it on your phone.
:::
